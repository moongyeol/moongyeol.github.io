Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation

TMS

Alongside medication, exploring the possibility of another treatment

TMS (Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation) is a non-invasive brain stimulation treatment that uses a magnetic field generated by a coil to stimulate specific regions of the brain.

Without cutting the skin or skull, it delivers magnetic stimulation to the cerebral cortex, which can influence the activity of nerve cells.

Through repeated stimulation, it aims to regulate the activity of specific neural circuits.

How does Moongyeol approach TMS?

TMS is not a treatment applied to every mental health issue.

At Moongyeol, we comprehensively evaluate the diagnosis and symptoms, response to prior treatment, the limitations of medication, and the patient's treatment preferences before determining whether TMS is an appropriate treatment.

It can be considered as a non-drug treatment option especially for depression and treatment-resistant depression.

You may want to discuss TMS if:

Depression

Persistent low mood, reduced motivation, and diminished interest

Treatment-resistant depression

Symptoms that haven't improved sufficiently despite adequate medication

Limitations with medication

A need to review other treatment options in light of medication's effectiveness, side effects, or your treatment preferences

A need to combine with existing treatment

Considering an additional non-invasive treatment while continuing current medication and psychotherapy

How does TMS work?

1

Magnetic field generation

A brief magnetic field is generated by a coil positioned close to the head.

→
2

Cortical stimulation

The magnetic field passes through the scalp and skull, inducing electrical changes in nerve cells at the target region.

→
3

Neural circuit regulation

Repeated stimulation aims to regulate the activity of neural circuits related to mood.

→
4

Repeated treatment

TMS is given repeatedly at a set frequency and interval, rather than as a single stimulation.

What does it feel like during treatment?

During treatment you'll hear a sound from the coil, and may feel a light tapping sensation on the scalp. Depending on the stimulation site, muscles around the face or scalp may move briefly. Some people experience headache or scalp discomfort, and in rare cases seizures can occur, so we review your history and risk factors before treatment.

Do I need to stop my medication?

No. Starting TMS does not mean you should stop any medication you are taking on your own. Any change or discontinuation of medication must be discussed with the prescribing physician, and whether to combine TMS with your current treatment plan is decided accordingly.
