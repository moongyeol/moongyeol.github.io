Esketamine

Esketamine Therapy

Specialized treatment for treatment-resistant depression

When depression continues despite adequate treatment, it's natural to wonder,
“Am I simply not responding to treatment?”

In treatment-resistant depression, it's important to re-evaluate prior treatment and, if needed, add a treatment that works through a different mechanism.

Esketamine is a parenteral treatment option to consider for this kind of treatment-resistant depression.

The use of Esketamine nasal spray in adults with treatment-resistant depression is also specified in U.S. FDA approval.

When might it be considered?

Treatment-resistant depression

Insufficient improvement despite appropriate antidepressant treatment

Recurring depressive symptoms

Depressive symptoms that persist or recur despite adequate treatment

A need to re-evaluate prior treatment

When a new treatment strategy is needed after comprehensively evaluating the effectiveness and side effects of medication and treatment history

※ Whether Esketamine treatment is appropriate is decided by comprehensively evaluating the diagnosis, treatment history, and current condition.

How does Esketamine treatment proceed?

1

Preliminary evaluation

Checking current depressive symptoms, past treatment history, medications, and physical condition.

→
2

Treatment planning

Deciding together with your medical team whether Esketamine treatment is appropriate.

→
3

Administration

Administered according to a set method under medical supervision.

→
4

Observation

Your condition is checked under medical observation for a set period after administration.

→
5

Progress evaluation

Treatment response and side effects are checked, and the treatment plan is adjusted going forward.

What changes might occur after treatment?

After Esketamine administration, temporary drowsiness, dizziness, dissociation, elevated blood pressure, and nausea may occur.

For this reason, it is not a treatment where you return to daily activities immediately after administration, and medical observation is required. The FDA approval also specifies key warnings including sedation, dissociation, respiratory depression, and elevated blood pressure.

Do I need to stop my medication?

No. Esketamine treatment is given within the patient's overall treatment plan, and whether to adjust medication, including existing antidepressants, is decided by the medical team.
