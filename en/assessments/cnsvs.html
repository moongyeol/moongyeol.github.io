Computerized Neurocognitive Test

CNSVS

Objectively evaluating current cognitive function, from memory and attention to processing speed and executive function.

CNS Vital Signs (CNSVS) is a computerized neurocognitive test that objectively evaluates a range of cognitive functions using a computer.

Rather than simply checking memory, it comprehensively looks at multiple cognitive domains — memory, attention, processing speed, reaction time, executive function, cognitive flexibility, and motor speed.

At MoonGyeol, we use CNSVS to objectively check the overall status and domain-specific characteristics of current cognitive function, and comprehensively judge your current condition together with the clinical evaluation and any additional testing needed.

When might this test be considered?

When memory or cognitive function seems different from before

Frequently forgetting recent conversations or important appointments, or feeling that your thinking has slowed compared to before

When concentration is declining

Finding it hard to focus on tasks, making more mistakes, or being easily distracted

Checking the cognitive characteristics of ADHD

When it's necessary to look at processing speed, executive function, and response inhibition alongside attention

When depression, anxiety, or stress seem to be affecting cognition

Wanting to objectively confirm whether declines in memory and concentration reflect an actual cognitive problem or changes accompanying your emotional state

When lack of sleep or fatigue is reducing concentration

Needing to check how sleep problems or chronic fatigue are affecting attention and processing speed

After brain injury or a neurological condition

Needing to objectively compare cognitive changes before and after treatment or over the course of the condition

When worried about memory decline or dementia

Wanting to objectively check current cognitive function, with a screening assessment that can lead to more precise neurocognitive evaluation if needed

What does it evaluate?

CNSVS evaluates several cognitive domains individually, helping identify the strengths and weaker areas of your current cognitive function.

Composite memory

The ability to comprehensively remember verbal and visual information

Verbal memory

The ability to learn and remember words and language information

Visual memory

The ability to remember and recognize visual information

Psychomotor speed

The speed of performing cognitive processing together with a motor response

Reaction time

The time it takes to recognize and respond to a stimulus

Complex attention

The ability to attend to multiple stimuli and regulate unnecessary responses

Cognitive flexibility

The ability to shift thinking and responses to match changing rules or situations

Processing speed

The ability to process information and respond quickly

Executive function

Abilities related to planning, shifting, inhibition, and problem-solving

Simple attention

The basic ability to focus on and respond to a specific stimulus

Motor speed

The speed of motor responses such as finger movement

Results explained by the medical team

The test is performed by completing each task while looking at a computer screen.

Performance in each domain is compared against age-based norms and can be viewed as standard scores and percentiles.

This isn't a test with just one score

Cognitive function isn't made up of a single ability. Some people may have good memory but slower processing speed, while others may struggle more with attention or executive function than with memory.

Depression, anxiety, lack of sleep, fatigue, and ADHD can also each affect different cognitive domains in different ways.

So at MoonGyeol, rather than judging by a single overall score, we look at domain-specific results together with current symptoms and everyday functioning.

How are results used?

CNSVS can be used to objectively identify the characteristics of your current cognitive function. In particular, it helps check things like:

  • Whether memory decline is actually present
  • Whether there are difficulties with attention or processing speed
  • Whether there are weaknesses in executive function or cognitive flexibility
  • Which cognitive domain is relatively weaker among several
  • How cognitive function changes during treatment or follow-up

Where needed, results can be used as a basis for further, more precise neurocognitive or neuropsychological testing.

Can CNSVS diagnose dementia?

CNSVS is not a test that diagnoses dementia on its own. It objectively evaluates the current state of cognitive function, and when dementia or mild cognitive impairment is suspected, the determination is made by combining history, symptoms, everyday functioning, neurological evaluation, and any additional necessary tests.

Conversely, it isn't a test used only when dementia is a concern. It can be used in a variety of situations where it's necessary to check characteristics such as attention, memory, processing speed, and executive function.

Test duration

About 20–30 minutes. CNSVS's basic Brief-Core battery is designed to evaluate several cognitive domains within a short time, and generally takes about 20–30 minutes.
