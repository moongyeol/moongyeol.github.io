Comprehensive Attention Test

CAT

Attention isn't a single thing. We look at multiple domains of attention together.

CAT (Comprehensive Attention Test) is a computerized test that evaluates attentional ability across multiple domains through your responses to various stimuli.

Rather than simply confirming whether “concentration is good or bad,” it comprehensively looks at your response to visual and auditory stimuli, your ability to sustain attention, and your ability to inhibit unnecessary responses.

At MoonGyeol, we interpret CAT results together with your clinical evaluation, history, and everyday difficulties to understand the nature of any attention problem and decide on a treatment direction.

When might this test be considered?

Suspected adult ADHD

Difficulty concentrating, being easily distracted, or procrastinating and struggling to finish tasks

Feeling that concentration has declined

Frequent mind-wandering while studying or working, or difficulty sustaining attention for long periods

Difficulty with impulsivity or attention control

Acting before thinking, making frequent mistakes, or finding it hard to wait

Checking change before and after ADHD treatment

Where needed, this can be used to objectively compare attention before and after treatment.

What does it evaluate?

CAT evaluates various aspects of attention through several types of tasks.

Visual attention

Evaluates the ability to accurately distinguish visual stimuli on screen and respond appropriately to relevant stimuli.

Auditory attention

Looks at the ability to accurately hear and distinguish stimuli presented as sound, and respond appropriately.

Sustained/inhibitory attention

Evaluates the ability to sustain attention continuously while inhibiting unnecessary or inappropriate responses during a task.

Selective/interference attention

Evaluates the ability to suppress the influence of unnecessary information and selectively attend to needed information when multiple stimuli are presented at once.

Divided attention

Evaluates the ability to appropriately allocate attention and respond while processing multiple pieces of information or tasks at the same time.

Working attention

Evaluates the ability to briefly hold presented information, select what's needed, and use it to perform a task.

How are CAT results used?

CAT is one reference point for diagnosing ADHD.

Results alone do not diagnose or rule out ADHD — the onset of symptoms, everyday difficulties, history, clinical interview, and other necessary evaluations are all considered together.

Where needed, it can be used to compare changes before and after medication or to adjust the direction of treatment.

FAQ

Q. Can CAT alone diagnose ADHD?

No. CAT is a test that objectively evaluates characteristics of attention, while an ADHD diagnosis is made by combining clinical interview, history, symptoms, and other assessment results.

Q. Can adults take this test too?

Yes. CAT can be used to evaluate attention in adults as well as children and adolescents.

Q. If the results are normal, does that mean it's not ADHD?

Not necessarily. ADHD isn't determined by a single test result — the symptoms and functional difficulties that show up in real life are evaluated together.
