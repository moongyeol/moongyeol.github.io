Heart Rate Variability Test

HRV

Looking at autonomic nervous system status and the body's response to stress

Our bodies constantly adjust their state — tensing when tension is needed, and recovering again when rest is needed.

Heart Rate Variability (HRV) is a test that analyzes the tiny variations in timing between heartbeats to look at how the autonomic nervous system is regulating itself and the characteristics of your physiological response.

At MoonGyeol, we use HRV to check your current autonomic nervous system status and look at how your body responds to stress and relaxation.

How does the autonomic nervous system work?

Our autonomic nervous system regulates functions like heart rate, breathing, and blood pressure without any conscious effort.

Sympathetic nervous system — the system for activity and tension

In situations of stress or threat, it works to raise heart rate and arousal so the body can respond quickly.

Parasympathetic nervous system — the system that supports rest and recovery

In stable situations, it lowers heart rate and bodily arousal, helping rest and recovery take place.

What matters isn't just whether one side is ‘higher or lower.’ The autonomic nervous system changes flexibly with circumstances, tensing when needed and then recovering again. So rather than interpreting HRV as simply “sympathetic activity is high” or “parasympathetic activity is low” from a single number, it needs to be considered together with the state at the time of measurement and various physiological factors.

Stress and the autonomic nervous system

Stress itself is a normal bodily response. But when tension and stress repeat without enough recovery, changes can appear in the body's patterns of arousal and recovery. This can show up differently from person to person.

Anxiety and tension

The body may tense easily and react sensitively even to small stimuli.

Sleep problems

Difficulty falling asleep or frequent waking can make it hard to get enough rest.

Fatigue and lethargy

Ongoing stress combined with lack of sleep can lead to fatigue and difficulty recovering.

However, these symptoms aren't caused by the autonomic nervous system alone. Brain function, emotional state, sleep, lifestyle, and physical factors all interact and contribute.

What does HRV look at?

HRV analyzes variation in the interval between heartbeats in several ways.

Heart rate

Checks the average heart rate during measurement.

HRV indices

Analyzes the degree of variation between heartbeats to look at characteristics of autonomic regulation.

Time-domain analysis

Analyzes variability in heartbeat intervals from a time perspective.

Frequency-domain analysis

Analyzes the frequency characteristics of HRV to look at patterns of autonomic regulation.

Changes during measurement

Physiological responses at rest and changes during a relaxation process can be referenced.

※ HRV indices can be influenced by various factors such as measurement time, posture, breathing, physical condition, and exercise, so a comprehensive interpretation is needed.

When HRV assessment may be considered

  • When your body tenses easily under stress
  • When a state of anxiety or heightened sensitivity persists
  • When palpitations or physical symptoms appear during tension
  • When physical tension doesn't release even after enough rest
  • When sleep quality declines or fatigue persists
  • Wanting to objectively check your own body's response to stress
  • Wanting to check changes before and after Biofeedback treatment

How are HRV results used?

HRV is not a test that determines your health or mental health status from a single number.

At MoonGyeol, we interpret HRV results comprehensively together with your current symptoms, sleep status, stress level, lifestyle, and clinical evaluation.

Where needed, HRV can also be measured repeatedly before and after treatment to reference changes in physiological response.

HRV and Biofeedback

While the HRV test is an assessment that checks your current autonomic nervous system status and physiological response, Biofeedback is a treatment that trains you to regulate your body's physiological responses — through breathing, relaxation, and more — while checking your own physiological signals in real time.

So at MoonGyeol, where needed, HRV assessment results can be used to plan Biofeedback treatment and track its progress.

FAQ

Q. Does low HRV mean I have severe stress?

Not necessarily. HRV is influenced by many factors besides stress — age, activity level, sleep, breathing, posture, and physical condition, among others. So stress level isn't judged from a single number alone.

Q. Can HRV diagnose anxiety or depression?

HRV alone cannot diagnose a specific mental health condition. It is a supportive assessment that looks at the physiological state of the autonomic nervous system, and is interpreted together with a psychiatric clinical evaluation and symptom assessment.

Q. Why is an HRV test needed?

Its value lies in letting you check, through objective indicators, physiological responses that are otherwise hard to notice in everyday life. In particular, it can help look at how stress or tension shows up in the body, and can be a reference point for planning non-drug treatments such as Biofeedback where appropriate.

※ HRV is a supportive assessment method that looks at autonomic nervous system status and physiological response. Results alone do not diagnose a condition — they are interpreted together with a clinical evaluation and symptom assessment by the medical team.
